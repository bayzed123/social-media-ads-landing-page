Use motion graphics, a talking avatar built from the supplied photo/voice, and a price-reveal slider transition. Every scene needs on-screen captions — most viewers watch muted.

## What Actually Makes a Reels Ad Perform (build these in, don't skip them)
- **Frame 1 has to stop the thumb** — the discount/price should be visible or implied in the first second, not saved for a slow reveal.
- **Hook in 0–2 seconds**, before any logo or branding.
- **Native vertical framing**, not a letterboxed horizontal video.
- **Burned-in captions** on every line of speech and every key claim.
- **One message, one CTA** — don't try to list every speaker feature; pick 2–3 and move fast.
- **A loopable/satisfying final beat** (the price-slash or the speaker turning on) — Reels with a clean loop get rewatched, and rewatches help distribution.

**Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:03)**
Fast zoom-in on the JBL Flip 6, bold animated text slams onto screen: "৳6,000 → ৳2,990" with the old price visibly struck through mid-motion. No voiceover yet — pure motion + text impact.

**Scene 3 — Product Demo / Motion Graphics (0:08–0:18)**
Dynamic product shots (use the supplied JBL Flip 6 photos/video) with animated feature callouts popping in one at a time, synced to quick cuts:

**Scene 4 — Price-Reveal Slider (0:18–0:23)**

**Scene 5 — CTA (0:23–0:28)**
Large animated "Order Now" button pulses in, Gaget Park logo, product page reference (gagetpark.com). Avatar's final line, direct to camera:

End on a clean, loopable final frame — the speaker sitting next to the price badge, static for the last half-second so it reads well even paused on.

## Avatar & Voice Direction
- Casual, energetic, trustworthy — a friend telling you about a deal, not a formal salesperson.
- Keep each avatar line short (one breath, under ~8 words of English-equivalent length) — long avatar monologues feel slower and read as less native to the Reels format.
- If voice-cloning from a supplied sample, keep the pacing a little faster than natural conversation — Reels viewers skip slow starts.

## On-Screen Text & Motion Style
- Bold, high-contrast sans-serif captions, positioned in the safe-zone (clear of where Instagram/Facebook overlay their own UI — avoid the very top and bottom ~15% of frame).
- Keep the brand accent color consistent across every text callout and the final CTA button, so it reads as one cohesive ad, not stitched clips.
- Transitions: quick wipes/slides, not slow fades — match the energy of the hook all the way through.
